- R2. Wang et al. (2024), *Large Language Models are not Fair Evaluators*, ACL 2024. https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.511/
- R3. Shi et al. (2025), *Judging the Judges: A Systematic Study of Position Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge*, IJCNLP-AACL 2025. https://aclanthology.org/2025.ijcnlp-long.18/

- R8. Zheng et al. (2023), *Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena*. https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685
- R9. NIST (2023), *Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)*. https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.100-1
- R10. Ye et al. (2024), *Justice or Prejudice? Quantifying Biases in LLM-as-a-Judge*. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02736

Use notes:
- R1 motivates a controlled multi-bias benchmark rather than trusting overall evaluator correlation.
- R2/R3 motivate order swaps, repetition stability, position consistency and preference fairness.
- R4 motivates provenance-blind scoring and dedicated self-preference probes.
- R5 motivates language-specific calibration with qualified native speakers.
- R6 motivates repeated trials and explicit consistency metrics.
- R7 motivates symmetric framing pairs and wording regression tests.
- R8 documents position, verbosity and self-enhancement concerns in early LLM-judge practice.
- R9 supports lifecycle risk measurement, documented limitations and human accountability.
- R10 motivates a broad, perturbation-based bias catalogue.
