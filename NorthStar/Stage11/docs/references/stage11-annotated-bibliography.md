# Stage 11 Annotated Bibliography

**Verification date:** 2026-08-01. Primary and official sources are preferred. Status can change; reverify for implementation.

1. **NIST, Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0), NIST AI 100-1 (2023).** Official voluntary framework organized around Govern, Map, Measure and Manage. Used for governance and evidence-ownership structure. https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework
2. **NIST, Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework: Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile, NIST AI 600-1 (2024).** Cross-sector companion profile for generative-AI risks and actions. Used for GenAI-specific risk framing; not a certification. https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence
3. **ISO/IEC 42001:2023, Artificial intelligence — Management system.** Official ISO page describes requirements for establishing and continually improving an AI management system. Used for management-system and accountability concepts; full standard is licensed. https://www.iso.org/standard/42001
4. **ISO/IEC 23894:2023, Artificial intelligence — Guidance on risk management.** Official AI risk-management guidance. Used for integrating risk management into AI lifecycle activities. https://www.iso.org/standard/77304.html
5. **OWASP, Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026 (published 2025-12-09).** Peer-reviewed agentic-application risk guidance. Used as an evolving security crosswalk, not a standard or proof of security. https://genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-top-10-for-agentic-applications-for-2026/
6. **OWASP, Securing Agentic Applications Guide 1.0 (2025-07-27).** Practical technical recommendations for agentic application builders and defenders. https://genai.owasp.org/resource/securing-agentic-applications-guide-1-0/
7. **OWASP, Multi-Agentic System Threat Modeling Guide v1.0 (2025-04-23).** Adds multi-agent trust, coordination and propagation considerations. Used to justify keeping inactive future surfaces distinct. https://genai.owasp.org/resource/multi-agentic-system-threat-modeling-guide-v1-0/
8. **IETF/RFC Editor, RFC 9700 / BCP 240, Best Current Practice for OAuth 2.0 Security (2025-01).** Current OAuth security BCP updating earlier threat guidance. Used for token and receiver-security principles. https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9700/
9. **NIST SP 800-34 Rev. 1, Contingency Planning Guide for Federal Information Systems (2010).** Official contingency-planning guidance and business-impact/recovery process. Used as a planning reference; applicability must be contextualized. https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/34/r1/upd1/final
10. **Google, Site Reliability Engineering — Service Level Objectives and Error Budget Policy.** Primary SRE guidance connecting user-facing objectives, error budgets and release decisions. NorthStar adds an independent zero-tolerance control gate. https://sre.google/sre-book/service-level-objectives/ and https://sre.google/workbook/error-budget-policy/
11. **OpenTelemetry, Semantic Conventions 1.43.0 and GenAI observability guidance (verified 2026).** Provides common telemetry meaning; GenAI conventions remain fast-moving and content capture is opt-in and privacy-sensitive. https://opentelemetry.io/docs/specs/semconv/ and https://opentelemetry.io/blog/2026/genai-observability/
12. **FinOps Open Cost and Usage Specification, FOCUS, and FOCUS 1.4 guidance.** Open technical specification for normalized technology billing data; 1.4 adds invoice reconciliation and commitment detail. Used as the future provider-billing normalization target. https://focus.finops.org/focus-specification/ and https://www.finops.org/insights/introducing-focus-1-4/
13. **FinOps Foundation, FinOps for AI.** Official framework category connecting AI technology cost and value. Used to frame cost per successful business outcome rather than tokens alone. https://www.finops.org/framework/technology-categories/ai/
14. **Kubernetes documentation — autoscaling, resource management and PodDisruptionBudget.** Official runtime mechanics for capacity and availability design. These are deployment building blocks, not proof that a workload meets its SLO. https://kubernetes.io/docs/concepts/workloads/autoscaling/ ; https://kubernetes.io/docs/concepts/configuration/manage-resources-containers/ ; https://kubernetes.io/docs/tasks/run-application/configure-pdb/
15. **NorthStar cumulative stage packages and validation reports.** Project-specific sources for accepted identifiers, decisions, tests and limitations. They are local tutorial evidence and do not replace enterprise evidence.
